# Changelog

## 0.10.0

- Places at sea: the World Map has a new kind of place, Waters. In the Creator a click on the sea puts one down, and any place can be dragged out over the water. It is joined by sea lanes and marched to as any other place. On a map drawn over your own picture, pick Waters as the kind of the place.
- Battles on ships: the Map Generator makes a battlefield of Waters, or of open sea picked out on the World Map, as water with two ships lying side by side. Each has masts, crates to shelter behind and a helm at the stern, with boarding planks from one deck to the other. The battle is joined from the company's deck and leads to the other ship's helm. Any other kind of place put out at sea stands on an island of its own.
- Ships at Sea is a new kind of battlefield in the Map Generator, for a map made without the World Map.
- Deck is a new terrain of the terrain brush: the boards of a ship, a bridge or a jetty, which does all that Plain does. Bridges and the jetty of a port are drawn as boards now.

## 0.9.0

- Level ups come by themselves: a unit that reaches 100 EXP levels up a moment later on the screen of the player it belongs to, with its growth rolls and the Level Up splash, or its promotion where its class promotes. A unit no player owns levels up on the Game Master's screen, and one whose player is away waits until they join. The Level Up button of the sheet still works, and a world setting switches the automatic level up off.
- Level Sync: an encounter can be held to a band of Levels in the Encounter Builder. When its battle begins, each player unit above the band fights as it was at the band's highest Level, with the stats, HP, weapon ranks, class and skills of that Level, and is itself again when the battle ends. Nothing of the unit's own is lost, and its level ups wait until it is over. The builder shows how each unit that is going stands and reads difficulty from the synced numbers.
- Battlefields from the World Map: the Map Generator can make the battle map of a place, or of bare ground, on the World Map. Sea, high ground, woods and roads fall where the World Map shows them, and what stands at the place stands at the heart of the map: a town round a keep, a fortress, a village, a harbour or ruins. A Battlefield button on the Game Master's plate opens it, and a place's saved encounter is fought on the map made for it.
- Supports: partners now earn support points for what they do together in battle, and those who stand side by side gain theirs as the Player Phase ends. From A a support no longer climbs by itself: the partners choose between A+ and S, of which a unit holds one each. A heart pops over both partners whenever their support grows.
- A player's unit now stands on the map as itself: its token is linked to its sheet as the unit is made, handed to a player or chosen as a character. When a Game Master opens the world after the update, the units players already had are linked once.

## 0.8.0

- World Map, travel view: a new button beside the zoom puts the map over the whole screen with no window around it, under Foundry's own sidebar and controls as a scene would be. Buttons at its top right go back to a window or close it, and let the Game Master show the map to everyone or edit it. The map reopens the way each person last had it, and showing it to everyone from the travel view opens it that way for them.
- World Map, arrivals: when the company has marched to a place, its name, kind and nation are shown across every open map. Where a battle waits, the Game Master can begin it there and then or wait; at the Base anyone can go in; anywhere else the name clears by itself after a moment, or at a click.

## 0.7.0

- World Map: a map of the campaign's land with a dot for each place, routes between them and the ground of each nation. The Game Master sends the company from place to place and everyone with the map open watches it march; players see the places shown to them and can point at one. A place can hold the Base, a market and a battle from a saved encounter.
- World Map Creator: the Game Master makes a land from a shape, a number of nations and a seed, or draws over a picture, and changes borders, places and routes by hand. Both open from the Fire Emblem controls.
- Accessories are now worn, one at a time, from the sheet or the battle HUD, and give what their Effect says while they are. A weapon in hand and a Shield give what their Effects say as well: stat and combat bonuses, extra attacks, Weapon Triangle changes, healing on hit, and what a hit leaves on the foe. Gear that grants a skill gives its unit that skill for as long as it is equipped. When a Game Master opens the world after the update, a unit that carries accessories and wears none puts on the first of them.
- A weapon, an item, a Combat Art or a Battalion can be shown to the table as a card in the chat without using it, from the unit sheet, the item's own window and the lists of the battle HUD.
- Retreat zone: the Game Master marks the tiles units can leave the map from with the terrain brush, beside the deployment zone. It is drawn in yellow for everyone, before a battle and all through it. In a battle, a unit that stands on one of its tiles has the Retreat command: it is the unit's Action, and takes the unit off the map as one that retreated.
- A new setting of each player's own, "Map squares: how solid", thins out the squares that show where a unit can move and what it can reach, so that the map can be seen under them. Each square keeps its outline and a band of its colour.
- On the battle map: a right click on the ground calls off what was being picked, or deselects the unit. Until a battle begins, a selected unit has no reach painted, so that units are set up with nothing in the way. A drag begun with Ctrl held puts a token anywhere. The terrain card names the ground under the pointer with no unit selected, and holding Alt shows the ground of the map. No turn ring is drawn in a battle played in Phases.
- At the Base, a player's own units are theirs to lead, and an ally says who runs it: the Game Master hands it to anyone at the table, and a player can take one nobody runs. The character a Game Master has chosen counts as a player's unit.

## 0.6.0

- NPC Builder: the Game Master makes an enemy, an ally or a bystander from a class, a Level and a side. Its base stats, Level ups, promotions, skills and weapon ranks are filled in as the rules give them, and anything can be changed by hand. A unit can be marked as a Boss or as recruitable, what it carries can be set to drop for the players or for anyone, and a unit set to vary gives each copy its own Level ups when it is placed.
- Encounter Builder: a battle is prepared ahead of the session and kept with the world: its map, Objectives, Map Affinity and weather, which units are in it and how many, where they stand, and the turn they arrive on. One button places it on the map, and another begins the battle and tells the players their Objectives.
- The Encounter Builder reads a battle against the party that is going, picked from every player unit in the world: how hard it looks, from Easy to Lunatic, the turns it takes to clear, the Kill EXP on the map, and how many hits each foe and each unit needs to fell the other. The difficulty is a rough guide of this system's own, not a rule of the game.
- Units that arrive on a later turn appear on the map and join the battle as that turn begins, and what a defeated unit leaves behind is told in chat.
- Both builders open from the Fire Emblem controls, beside the Map Generator.

## 0.5.0

- Skills on units made under an older version are brought up to date by themselves. When a Game Master opens the world after an update, every skill takes its Effect, its numbers, what the system does with it and its icon from the skills compendium, and keeps what is the unit's own: whether it is equipped, what was picked on it, and where its cooldown and uses stand. The Game Master is told in chat which skills changed, which now ask for a pick, and which weapon ranks to check by hand. Skills from a compendium of your own are left alone.
- Level Up splash: a level up is shown over the whole screen with the new level, the stats and what each gained, and a line from the unit. The lines for a great, a fair and a poor level are written on the Story tab of the sheet. Each player can switch the splash off for themselves.
- Chat cards are shorter: a card shows its headline and its results, and the magnifying glass on the card, or a right-click on the message, opens everything behind them, down to the dice as they fell.
- Optional Content: Katanas are in the game as a kind of Sword with a place of their own in the Weapon Triangle, along with the classes Ashigaru, Rei, Hitokiri, Oni Savage, Yokai, Shugoki, Merchant, Profiteer and Tycoon and their twelve skills, among them Income, Haggle, Hard to Kill and Wealth Of Power.
- Deployment zone: the Game Master marks the tiles the players set up on with the terrain brush. It is shown to everyone, and units stay inside it until the battle begins.
- Skill Capacity uses the raised values for all units: 30 Capacity Points, 60 from the second tier and 120 from the third.
- Heart Seal Classes are chosen from a menu on the sheet that lists every class a unit can start in, with a field to search them.
- A unit that waits for help in the Character Creator carries a raised hand beside its name in the Actors list.
- A new setting limits the Gold a skill such as Income gives in one map.

- The whole Skills list has been gone through, and nearly every skill is now applied by the system: in the forecast, in the fight, on the map and as turns pass. A skill that hangs on something the system cannot see says so on its sheet and is named in a fight to be applied by hand.
- New commands on the battle HUD: Shove and the skills that push, pull and swap units, Rescue and Drop, Refresh with its songs and dances, Steal, and Trade.
- Canto and its kin let a unit move again after acting, and skills such as Galeforce give it another go in the same turn.
- Shield Block: a Shield is equipped beside the weapon and blocks the hits it can, with its Block chance in the forecast and on the combat card.
- Skills that are chosen before a fight are switched on from the forecast, and skills that answer in the middle of one (dodge a hit, roll a miss again, soak a hit) ask their player with one click. A new setting lets the Game Master's units answer by themselves.
- Fights follow the skills that reshape them: attack order, extra attacks, further rounds, the Weapon Triangle, hits that splash onto the units around, and what a fight leaves on both sides afterwards.
- Skills used as an action are used from the battle HUD by picking a unit, a tile or an area on the map, and stances such as guarding, or stepping in for an ally, last until something ends them.
- Summons beyond the Phantom: undead minions, animals, Spirits, Mogalls, Terrors and reinforcements appear as units of their own, and go when their skill says so.
- Imitate takes on another unit's attributes for a few turns, and skills that lend a weapon put it in the unit's hands for the attacks it starts.
- Staves that reach several units, or reach further, now do, and more staff effects are applied by themselves.
- Doors, chests and Villages can be placed on a map by the Game Master and acted on by units; Mines and Traps are laid on tiles and spring on whoever steps on them.
- Weather: the Game Master sets the weather of a map from the Phase banner, and its movement costs, stat changes, damage, lightning and ballista fire are applied. Fog of War itself is left to the Game Master.
- Map Affinity: a map can have an Affinity of its own, and units of that Affinity gain Hit and Dodge on it.
- Additional Affinity Effects: all of them are applied, the ones a unit uses when it wants are switched on from its sheet or the battle HUD, and the rank or shorter cooldown some of them give is chosen on the sheet.
- Skill cut-ins: as a skill takes effect its icon and name flash over its unit, with a sound. Each player can set them to fast or off and mute the sound for themselves.
- A side with no units left in the fight no longer takes a Phase.
- Experience can be given to a unit from its sheet, multiplied by its skills and those of the units around it.

## 0.3.0

- Six more Optional Modules, each a world setting that starts switched off: Stagger, Passive Staff Effects, Heal Criticals, Double-Cast, Staff Missing and the Follow-up Critical Multiplier.
- Stagger gives every unit a Stagger bar beside its HP, on the sheet, the battle HUD and the forecast. A unit whose bar is emptied is Staggered: it takes double damage and cannot act until the end of its next turn.
- The Follow-up Critical Multiplier is set for each unit on the Optional tab of its sheet, and shows in the forecast and on the combat card.
- Summoning: a unit with Summon puts its Phantom on a free tile next to it, with a weapon drawn for its level. The Phantom is a unit of its own that keeps to its summoner's level, and Multi-Phantom allows more than one on the map.
- Warping: Teleport and Agile Teleport let a unit pick a tile to warp to from the battle HUD, and the staves that move units (Warp, Rescue, Rewarp, Entrap, Push, Return, Miswarp) now move the token themselves.
- Trainees are created as the rules have them: no Generic Skill, every proficiency at E Rank and fewer growth points to place. On promoting, a Trainee takes its Generic Skill from the Skill Roll.
- A class can start one proficiency at a higher rank than D, as "1 at C rank" says, and the Creator follows it.
- Skills that give weapon ranks, such as Aspected and Practiced, raise the rank when taken and give it back when removed.
- Skills that come in several variants, or that raise growth rates of the unit's choosing, are chosen in the Creator.
- More skills are applied automatically in combat, among them Disarm, Ruin, Black Magic, Cudgel, Flense, Inferno, Fearsome, Flash Freeze, Dragon Ward, Anathema, Breath of Life and Prevail.
