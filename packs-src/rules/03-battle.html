Now that you know what your stats do, it is time to get into what you can do. All units have a variety of Actions available to them: Wait, Attack, Use, Shove, Rescue, Seize and more.

Key rule

On its turn a unit may move, then take one Action. Taking an Action ends the unit's turn, and all Skills that are “as an Action” do the same. A few things are free and do not: moving, equipping, Trading and Shifting.

Moving

MOV determines how far a unit can move in a turn. Units move in the cardinal directions, but not diagonally. Units can pass through their allies, but not enemies.

Movement can be freely done and reconsidered until the unit takes an Action and locks in, however other units cannot act while a unit is mid-movement.

Actions that end your turn

Wait
Simply ends the unit's turn.
Attack
Initiates combat with an enemy in range. See [[combat]].
Use
Use an Item, such as a Vulnerary. Units are able to Use Items they would use on themselves on adjacent allies, as well. Units gain 10 EXP when Using an Item on an ally.
Shove
Pushes another unit one space forward. Units can only Shove units who do not have 3 or more WT than them, and can Shove units of all WT values below that.
Rescue
Picks up an ally and brings them under your protection. See below.
Drop
A Rescuer can Drop their passenger as an action.
Open
Units can Open doors and chests, provided they have the means to, such as a key or the ability to pick locks.
Objective
Units may perform various objective-based Actions at certain GM-designated spots, such as Seizing a throne, Arriving at a destination, Escaping from the battlefield, and so on. See [[objectives]].

Rescuing

Rescuing picks up an ally and brings them under your protection, but halves your DEX and SPD. Rescued units cannot act or be targeted, only the Rescuer. Units can only Rescue allies with equal or less WT than them.

The unit who starts combat is the initiator. Both the initiator and defender get one attack each, the initiator attacking first with the defender counter-attacking after them.

The order of attacks

  1. The initiator attacks
  2. The defender counter-attacks

    The defender may only counter-attack if their weapon has sufficient Range. See [[weapons|Range and Midrange]].

  3. Whoever is faster attacks again

    If one of the two units has 4 or more Attack Speed than the other, they can make a second attack, called a follow-up or double.

    • If the initiator can double, they make their first attack, the defender counters, then the initiator doubles.
    • If the defender can, the initiator makes their attack, then the defender makes two counter-attacks.

Making an attack

Rolling to hit

To actually make an attack, the attacker rolls a D100. Whether or not this hits is determined by the Accuracy and Dodge of the units, with the defender's Dodge reducing the attacker's Accuracy.

Chance to hit
Accuracy − Foe's Dodge

If an attacker has 100 Accuracy and the defender has 50 Dodge, the attacker has a 50% final chance to hit.

Damage dealt
Might + STR or MAG − Foe's DEF or RES

The unit's relevant stat plus their weapon's Might, reduced by their foe's relevant defensive stat.

Key rule

If the attacker rolls equal to or below the final chance to hit, their attack connects and deals damage.

Critical Hits

When attacking, units also have a chance to Critically hit. Critical Hits deal 3× final damage, and are similarly a D100 roll, rolled alongside or after Hits.

Chance to crit
Critical − Foe's Critical Avoid

Rolling equal to or lower than the chance means a Critical Hit.

Skills

Finally, many Skills have a chance to activate when you make an attack. As with hitting and critting, rolling equal to or below your activation chance means you activate the Skill. See [[skills]].

Offensive Staves

For Offensive Staves, hitting is the same, just with Offensive Staff Hit against Offensive Staff Avoid. See [[combat-stats]].

After each hit

  • The weapon may lose a Use, depending on its kind. See [[weapons|Uses]].
  • The attacker adds the weapon's wEXP to their Proficiency, doubled on a killing blow. See [[weapons|Proficiency]].
  • If the weapon's Effect names a Condition, the target gains it. See [[conditions]].

Once the combat is over, the unit earns Experience for it. See [[exp]].

Ruling · Book of Stipulations

When a weapon attacks with an atypical damage stat for its type, it targets: Resistance if it uses MAG instead of STR, Resistance if it uses STR instead of MAG, Defense if it uses DEX instead of STR, and Defense if it uses Attack Speed instead of STR.

The Weapon Triangle is essentially combat Rock-Paper-Scissors. One weapon type has an advantage against another, and a disadvantage against a third. There is also the Magic Triangle and the Anima Pentagon.

Who beats whom

Each arrow points from the winner to the loser.

Source note

The pentagon includes Earth and Water, but the rules name only Fire, Wind and Thunder as the subcategories that Anima beats. The original wording is kept here.

What advantage gives you

The unit with Weapon Triangle Advantage gains bonuses to their accuracy and damage, and their opponent receives the same numbers as penalties.

Key rule

The size of the bonus is set by the Rank of the unit with advantage, in the weapon they are using. The losing side's Rank does not matter.

The plus goes to the unit with advantage, the minus to their opponent.

Outside the triangle

Weapons not included in a Weapon Triangle have neither an advantage nor a disadvantage against any other weapons, and weapons in the Triangle do not normally have an advantage or disadvantage against themselves.

Condition is what state a unit is currently in. This is usually “Fine” or “Normal”, but units can be inflicted with various negative status effects, or Conditions.

Key rule

Conditions last a set number of turns, but can be reapplied to reset (or in some cases, increase) the time. They can also be cured by various items, staves, and Skills.

If a weapon has the name of a Condition in its Effect, it inflicts that Condition on hit.

Ruling · Book of Stipulations

When a unit qualifies for damage, healing and a buff at the start of its turn, they resolve in the order Healing, Damage, Buffs, Debuffs. A unit would heal from Imbue, then be damaged by Poison, and then receive the buffs of White Pool.
