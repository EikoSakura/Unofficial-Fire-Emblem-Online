The rules of

Fire Emblem Online

Rules 3.0, by Erinys and contributors

Fire Emblem Online is, like Fire Emblem, a tactical role-playing game. Players form a party of units and play through the maps, battles and scenarios presented by the Game Master, the GM.

What kind of game this is

Fire Emblem Online is, first and foremost, just Fire Emblem. It is not a modification of D&D or Pathfinder or anything else. If you have existing familiarity with the mechanics of Fire Emblem, then learning will be easy. If not, hopefully it will still be easy.

Cooperation and communication are vital to the efficacy of your team, same with properly managing your resources. A party typically consists of 4 to 8 units, but this number can vary as much as the GM pleases, and you may not always be able to deploy every unit on your team. Parties can be any size in terms of players, too, and perhaps you may have to make multiple characters for a campaign.

There is no pre-defined world in Fire Emblem Online, or a basic lore; it is free for the GM to decide. Beorc are a constant, but they may not always be called that, and perhaps Laguz, or Manakete, or Taguel, or something else, will live in the world alongside them.

Fire Emblem Online is, like almost all tabletop role-playing games, a cooperative experience. Not just on the player side, but on the GM's side. The GM typically controls most if not everything that is not the player team, and so is responsible for the lives of their players' units.

GM advice

The system is particularly homebrew-friendly and adaptable. Game Masters are encouraged to mold the game to their needs, not necessarily by fiat, but by simply side-stepping restrictions and generating new content. Would the boss benefit from a skill that is impossible or improbable for them to obtain? Give it to them. Would the players enjoy a few new weapons tailored to their units' needs? Give them to them.

The goal is not to kill the players' units, but to present a story to them, a story with action and adventure, and perhaps tragedy and tension. You can still present a meatgrinder if you want, but keep in mind whether that is what they also want. Establish expectations before even getting your first volunteer for your game.
