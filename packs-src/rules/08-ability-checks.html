Ability Checks are a version of mechanics like Skill Checks from D&D or Pathfinder, reimagined and retooled to fit with Fire Emblem Online better. With them, the options for out-of-combat play expand greatly.

Key rule

Roll a D100 and add your bonus. If the result is equal to or over the target number, the attempt was successful. This is the reverse of combat, where you want to roll low.

Charm and Intelligence

At character creation, the unit chooses two stats to serve as the basis for their CHA and INT.

Key rule

An ability may only have an amount of points in it equal to 5 + your current level.

The three kinds of check

Regular Ability Check
This is used most of the time. Whenever a unit wishes to do something the GM deems worthy of a check, usually when a battle is not being fought (though depending on the action a GM may allow it to be attempted mid-combat), the player will roll D100 and add the bonus from the appropriate ability to the result. If the result is equal to or over the target number set by the GM, the attempt was successful.
Opposed Ability Check
The target number is not set by the GM beforehand but is instead the total of the responding Ability Check. For example, if person A was trying to lie to person B, the target number for A's Deception check would be B's Intuition check. If B rolled higher than A did, they did not believe the lie.
Saving Throw
In certain events, players may be required to perform a Saving Throw. This will usually occur when the players are in some sort of danger that does not involve combat, or the players are unaware of a danger and are reacting to it.

Every ability, what it is used for and how its bonus is worked out is listed in [[abilities]].

Setting a target number

GM advice

To determine the target number for a check, decide on what the success rate should be. As the game is played using D100s, a check with a target number of 50 should have a success rate of 50%, and a target number of 75 should have a success rate of 25% (100 − target number).

However, since players have bonuses added to their rolls, the success rates are actually a bit higher. For lower-level parties the bonuses are rather small, but as levels become higher these bonuses increase with them. Therefore it is a good idea to look at what bonuses the party has and work from there.

As opposed to combat, the GM does not need to tell players the likelihood of success, merely hint at the difficulty if desired.

Worked exampleA 60% chance for a +25 bonus

A player has a bonus of +25 in Acrobatics, and the GM would like their success rate to be 60%.

Target number
25 + (100 − 60)
65

Note that a player with an Acrobatics bonus of +10 would have a 45% chance of success with this check, should multiple people attempt it.
