In Fire Emblem, for the longest time, when the HP of a unit reached zero, it died. In a tabletop game, where a character is one you have worked hard on and had a journey with alongside others, that does not always feel so good.

GM advice

It is vital to establish to players, as GM, how you wish to handle Death, as there are a number of ways of handling it. Settle this before play begins.

Four ways to handle it

  1. Permadeath

    Permadeath, through and through. When a unit's HP reaches zero, it dies.

  2. Casual Mode

    Units retreat instead of die.

  3. Multiple lives

    Units have a number of lives to lose before they are gone for good. See the [[opt-lives]] Optional Module.

  4. Incapacitation

    Incapacitation is a bit more complex, but also provides a more fair in-between of permadeath and not dying, giving units a chance to get back up after reaching 0 HP and allowing players to choose their fate. See [[incapacitation]].

Under the Incapacitation system, a unit that is defeated goes down for a few turns instead of dying, and carries the Wounds and Injuries of battle with it afterwards.

GM advice

This page applies only if your GM has chosen Incapacitation as the way to handle death. See [[death]].

Wounds

When a character takes their maximum HP divided by 1.5 in damage from a single hit, they take a Wound.

  • Wounds decrease your maximum HP by one, and linger between maps until healed.
  • For every multiple of 5 (5, 10, 15, and so on) of Wounds you have, you gain a Grave Wound, and your STR, MAG, DEF, RES, DEX, SPD, LUK, MOV and CON decrease by 1, all to a minimum of 1.

Healing Wounds

Wounds are healed by another unit using their action to make a Medicine check, base DC 60, which increases by 5 for each Wound you have. Out of combat, this action takes 1 hour per Wound.

If the unit succeeds their Medicine check, you lose Wounds equal to the tens place of their Medicine ability. Units who succeed this check gain +5 EXP per Wound healed.

Going down

When a unit is defeated in combat, they become Incapacitated.

Rounds of Incapacitation
Endurance or Willpower ÷ 10

Grave Wounds decrease your total Incapacitation rounds by 1 per Grave Wound.

Overkill damage kept
Damage past 0 − Endurance or Willpower ÷ 10

This HP must be healed before the unit can be brought up.

While Incapacitated:

  • The unit no longer occupies their spot and is automatically hit by all attacks.
  • A unit may spend 1 round of Incapacitation to take one Action, or move half of their total movement.
  • If a unit with negative HP is healed, their Incapacitation timer is paused for 1 round, ending at the start of their next turn, or until they are hit.
  • A unit is allowed to take their action when brought back if they did not take it before they went down.

Injuries

When recovering from Incapacitation, the unit suffers an Injury. Each Injury lowers your maximum HP by 2.

Healing Injuries

Injuries are only healed out of combat, requiring a day of downtime doing nothing but healing, and another unit spending their time dedicated to healing you.

For each day spent like this, your healer rolls a Medicine check, base DC 70, which increases by 5 for every Injury you have. If they succeed, you lose Injuries equal to the tens place of their Medicine ability. Units who succeed this check gain +10 EXP per Injury healed. A unit may heal Wounds in the same check that they would be healing Injuries.

The final choice

When a unit reaches 5 Injuries, takes damage equal to 3× their Maximum HP before or while Incapacitated, or their Incapacitation timer runs out, they must choose one of the following options:

  1. Retreat

    They are forced to retreat, and leave the map. If the party succeeds in the map, they return to the party and may be treated as normal.

  2. Death

    The unit is killed.
