Fire Emblem Online is played on a square grid, with player units typically starting in a predefined area where they may rearrange themselves and assemble a formation before the map starts.

Objectives

Most maps have Objectives for the party to work towards, which include, but are not limited to:

Seize or Arrive
Have a player character choose the “Seize” or “Arrive” action on a certain space on the map.
Rout
Defeat all enemies on the map.
Defeat the Boss
Defeat a certain unit on the map, designated as the “Boss”.
Survive
Survive a certain number of turns.
Defend
Prevent enemies from ending their turn on a certain space on the map.
Protect
Prevent enemies from defeating a certain (usually ally) unit.
Escape
All players must choose the “Escape” action on a certain space on the map, leaving the map.

After completing all Objectives, the map typically ends.

Shops on the map

On the map, various shops may be found: Armories for weapons, Vendors for various items, Arenas for extra combat, and anything else the GM can come up with. Shop services are reached from House, Shop and Village terrain. See [[terrain]].

A map can be in any climate, whether it is on a mountaintop or in a desert. See [[weather]].

Certain terrain will impose movement restrictions on units, and may have additional effects.

Flying units can fly over most terrain and obstacles, ignoring both their penalties and positive effects, except for healing. See [[special-qualities]].

GM advice

Terrain can and should be used to control where units can move through the use of buildings, fences, rivers, walls, rock-faces, and countless other ways to manage movement. Flying units can mitigate these as per the GM's discretion; typically they can fly over most obstacles.

Elevation

Optionally, certain maps may also have terrain at different elevations at the GM's discretion and declaration.

Melee attacks cannot be made against targets at different elevations.

Doors are common obstacles in maps with buildings, and can be opened through the use of a key or a Skill, or through brute force.

Opening a door

A unit can Open a door as an Action, provided they have the means to, such as a key or the ability to pick locks. Otherwise it can be broken down: depending on the type of door, it will require a different amount of damage dealt to it in order to open.

Source note

This paragraph appears twice in the rules. One copy says doors open with “a key or the Pick skill”, the other with “a key or the Thievery skill”.

Maps may have Weather effects active, which typically disrupt and shake up gameplay for both sides.

Weather effects

Rain, Snowstorm
Tiles cost 1 more MOV to cross for infantry units, and 2 more for Mounted units.
Fog, Sandstorm, Darkness, Ash Storm
Causes Fog of War.
Mist
Causes Fog of War, but the LoS of all units is set to 4 unless it is higher.
Thunderstorm
Same effects as Rain, plus lightning. See below.
Windstorm
Tiles cost 1 more MOV to cross for Flying units.
Hail
Units take 1 damage at the end of their turn. Units cannot die this way.
Heat Wave
Units receive −3 SPD and take 1 damage at the end of their turn. Units cannot die this way. If the [[opt-fatigue]] Optional Module is being used, units also receive 1 fatigue.
Ballista Fire
Every other turn, 1D5 ballista bolts strike random tiles. Units struck by Ballistae take 10 damage. If one hits a tile considered indoors, nothing happens.

Weather effects can also be combined (ideally within reason) if the GM so chooses.

Lightning

In a Thunderstorm, at the end of every turn (after all phases are done and it is back to player phase), flip a coin. If it is tails, a bolt of lightning strikes a random tile.

  • If it hits a unit, it deals 10 damage.
  • If the unit has the Dragon Special Quality, it deals 15 instead.
  • If it hits a tile considered indoors, nothing happens, even if a unit was on that tile.

Picking a random tile

Where a lightning bolt or ballista bolt strikes can be determined by rolling the grid size of the map.

Worked exampleWhere the bolt lands

If the map was 10 × 10 (width, height) you would roll 2D10, and a result of 5, 5 would strike at 5 wide, 5 high.

If it was 10 × 15 you would roll a D10, then a D15.

In Foundry

Type /roll 1d10 and /roll 1d15 in chat for a map of that size.
