Two separate modules that build on Affinity: one adds more Affinities to choose from, the other gives every Affinity an innate effect.

Optional modules

These are two modules. A GM may use either one without the other. The core rules are in [[affinity]].

Optional Affinities

This module adds the following additional Affinities for character creation.

Endurance, or END, reduces all damage taken, like DEF and RES together. Haste, or HST, increases the ability to follow-up, without granting Dodge from increased Attack Speed.

Additional Affinity Effects

When this module is active, all units gain additional, innate effects from their Affinities. These effects are not given in Supports, and their effects are only for the unit.

Holy Blood is a multi-use mechanic which can be used for gameplay, roleplay and story purposes.

Optional module

Holy Blood is not meant to be acquirable mid-campaign by any unit. It must be gained at character creation, allowed or deliberately bestowed upon a character by the GM.

Minor Holy Blood

Units with Holy Blood gain substantial growth bonuses and, optionally, increases to their weapon ranks and the ability to use weapons that might be locked behind Holy Blood by the GM. Minor Holy Blood bestows the following bonuses.

Major Holy Blood

Major Holy Blood doubles the Holy Blood growth rate bonuses and increases the respective weapon rank to ★ Rank, granting SS weapon rank bonuses and allowing the unit to use the ★ Rank weapon of their Holy Blood type, designated or made by the GM.

GM advice

The example Holy Blood types are not entirely meant to be the end-all be-all of the system. GMs are heavily encouraged to come up with their own lineages of Holy Blood and unique bonuses should they wish to use Holy Blood in a campaign.

Crests bear a similar multi-purpose ability to Holy Blood, but their effects are considerably different. Crests provide activation abilities during combat, much like Skills.

Optional module

Units can only have one Crest, Minor or Major. Similar to [[opt-holy-blood]], Crests are not meant to be obtained mid-game and must be given to a player character at creation by the GM.

Crest effects

Each Crest has a chance to activate, higher for a Major Crest than for a Minor one.

GM advice

GMs are encouraged to come up with their own unique names (and possibly effects) for Crest bloodlines if they intend to use Crests in a campaign.

With this module enabled, all units pick three classes at character creation. Those three classes, plus their starting class, form a list of Heart Seal Classes that the unit can reclass into via Heart Seals.

Optional module

This adds a step to [[character-creation]]: choosing three more classes.

Reclassing

  1. Change your stats

    Add or subtract stats equal to the difference in base stats between the classes. If reclassing to a Mercenary from a Fighter, you would gain 3 DEF but lose 2 STR, and so on and so forth for all other stats, including CON, MOV, LoS and WT.

  2. Trade a Proficiency

    Lower a Proficiency your previous class had by 1, and raise a Proficiency gained by your new class by 1. If you gain multiple Proficiencies via a class, you gain and lose that amount respectively.

    • You may also choose not to gain any new Proficiencies from your class, in which case you do not lose any of your current Proficiencies.
    • Weapon Ranks decreasing via Reclassing cannot remove Proficiencies, only leave you with E Rank in them.
  3. Keep and gain Skills

    You retain any skills you had before your reclass, and gain any skills upon swapping, such as Starting Skills, Promotion Skills and Mastery Skills. You require an Occult Scroll to gain your new class's Occult Skill if you are not Level 50 or higher.

Gaining more classes

Whenever you reach A+ or S rank with another unit, pick one of their Heart Seal Classes to add to your own list. See [[supports]].

Ruling · Book of Stipulations

Outside of this reclassing system it is not really possible to lose a weapon rank.

Additional Lives are a mechanic meant to lessen the threat of permanent death.

Optional module

This is one of the four ways a GM can handle death. See [[death]].

How lives work

Simply put, player units (and some NPC ally units, if they are considered party members) have, by default, 3 lives. This number can be changed by the GM as they see fit.

  • If a unit's HP reaches 0, they lose a life instead of dying, and are no longer present for the battle as normal.
  • If they run out of all of their lives, they are permanently dead.

Gaining and restoring lives

PromotingGrants 1 more life total and restores 1. With default numbers, promoting to a Tier 3 class will give a unit a total of 5 lives.

Life CrystalsGive 1 additional total life, but do not restore any.

This module adds a unique currency gained from killing bosses, Glory, which can be used during downtime to unlock permanent upgrades for the character.

Optional module

Glory cannot be traded between units, and only the unit who scored the killing blow on a boss receives it. It is important to consider boss kills wisely, for both their Glory and their EXP.

Earning Glory

By default, bosses give 1 Glory on kill, but difficult or story-important bosses can and should give more.

Powers

The effects unlockable with Glory are known as Powers.

Source note

Deathly Affliction names a Stumble Condition, which is not defined in the core list or in [[opt-conditions]].

Five short modules that each change one part of how units grow. A GM may use any of them on its own.

Optional modules

The core rules these replace are in [[levelling]], [[weapons]] and [[skills]].

Manual Stat Gains

With this module, units may manually increase their stats. Instead of random growth chance, units have 3 points to spend when leveling up, and put these points into their stats to increase them.

  • Each stat can only be increased once per level.
  • If CON and MOV growth rates are present, it is up to GM discretion if and when these can be increased, but when they can be, they require all points.
  • GMs may freely alter the amount of points units get to spend to adjust unit power.

Optionally, growth rates may still be present, and serve an alternative function. Units only gain 125 Growth Points, and when a Growth rate procs, instead of increasing the stat, it opens it up for receiving 2 points instead of 1, thus increasing it by 2. It is completely optional for a unit to spend multiple points on a stat. Alternatively, growth rates may increase the stat gain to 2 using only 1 point.

Additional Growth Rates

Simply adds additional growth rates in the form of CON and MOV growth rates.

StatGrowth capStat cap
CON20%30
MOV5%20

GMs may also decide to raise the amount of growth rate points players get to accommodate these without compromising the growth rates of the player's primary stats. Additionally, CON and MOV growths do not increase by 5% when promoting.

Weapon Level

Weapon Level, or Wlv for short, is an alternative version of Weapon Ranks. Instead of gradually increasing through weapon use and by set ranks, Weapon Level is a growth rate, and weapons with a Weapon Level value equal to or lower than the unit's can be used.

  • This applies to all weapons the unit has proficiency in, but does not extend to other weapons. A Sword and Lance Paladin with a Wlv stat of 10 can wield Swords and Lances of 10 Wlv or lower, but cannot wield any Axes or other weapons unless they acquire the proficiency through means such as a Weapon Manual.
  • Training Weapons operate as normal, using wEXP to get from no rank to E rank. After you would reach E rank with a training weapon, you are considered proficient and will start using Wlv, and will not gain any more wEXP from using them.
  • Units start with 5 Wlv by default, but this can be lowered as desired by the GM, as well as increased by up to 2 like other stats using bonus points.
  • The cap for Weapon Level is equal to the highest Weapon Level value on the Reference Sheet, that being 30, and the cap for the growth rate is 70%, like most other growth rates.
  • Laguz, Manakete or Taguel will still automatically switch weapon upon leveling to the appropriate Wlv.
  • Weapon Rank Bonus will kick in at 20 and 25 Wlv for the S and SS Rank bonuses, respectively.

Source note

This module says most growth rates cap at 70%. The core rules cap growth rates at 80% for HP and 60% for all other stats.

Skill Capacity

Replaces the standard skill system with a capacity system, giving all units an expanded 9 skill slots restricted only by the unit's capacity.

The GM may at their discretion change Capacity Point values for specific classes or units, or apply it to all units, such as increasing the values to 30, 60 and 120. Each Skill's capacity cost is listed on the Reference Sheet.

Ruling · Book of Stipulations

If a unit no longer meets a Skill's prerequisite and the Skill is replaced, the replacement still has to follow the 5 to 10 Capacity rule of Beorc's Racial Skill.

Alternate Skill Activation

With this, all Skills will activate at either DEX% or LVL% instead of DEX/2%, depending on the GM's preference. Skills that use other stats (such as STR or SPD) will use the full stat if they did not already.
