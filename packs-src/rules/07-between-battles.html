Units can develop bonds and relations with each other and with GM designated NPCs (usually persistent allies), which provide statistical boosts and roleplay opportunities.

What a Support gives

At character creation, characters are given an [[affinity]], which determines what stat boosts they provide. Units gain these Affinity stat boosts when within a 3-space radius of each other.

There are two ways to determine support stat boosts. The GM chooses one.

Both Affinities
( Your boosts + Partner's boosts ) × Support Level

Units gain their own support bonuses, as well as gaining the bonuses of their partner.

Partner's Affinity only
Partner's boosts × Support Level

Alternatively, a GM may decide that a unit only gets the stat boosts of their partner's Affinity.

By default a unit gains the support bonuses of every unit they have a support with that is in range, but a GM instead may choose to have you only benefit from one support at a time, chosen freely during your turn.

Support ranks

There are 6 Support ranks, but only 5 grant bonuses: D C B A and A+ or S.

  • E Rank is less of a support rank and more a starting point for a support, as it does not give units Affinity stat boosts. E Rank may either be a mere designation of being pre-support, or need to be designated by both the player wishing to support another and the player they wish to support, and from there support points will start to gain.
  • You may only have one A+ Support and one S Support. S Supports do not need to be marital.
  • Supports can be cancelled or ended if both players consent to ending the support, if one of the supporting units dies, or in another circumstance decided by the GM, which will free up a slot.

Earning Supports

There are a few different methods of gaining supports.

Method 1: through roleplay

Support levels are gained as the units interact more in roleplay. As the units interact, the GM decides if the units will gain a support level after a conversation.

Method 2: a point for each battle together

Units gain a support point for every chapter or battle they are fielded together in. Support points are not reset upon gaining a support level when using this method. The support points required may vary depending on the GM, the units supporting each other, and other factors. You can use these requirements as a base:

Rank upSupport points
E to D2
D to C5
C to B10
B to A15
A to A+ or S20

Method 3: points for actions on the map

This method is the most complicated and tedious to track, and is not the recommended method. Units gain a support level upon gaining enough support points, with 80 being the default amount. Unlike the previous method, support points are reset upon gaining a support level.

ActionPoints

GM advice

GMs may change these values or set restrictions, like support points no longer increasing after a set number of turns, at their discretion, or use entirely different methods of tracking supports.

Bond Supports

Two players may ask the GM to have a Bond Support, which means their characters knew and interacted with each other before the campaign started.

  • Bond Supports start off at D rank rather than E rank.
  • Bond Supporting units gain a +10 bonus to Hit and Dodge and a +5 bonus to Critical when adjacent to each other, along with their other Affinity-related boosts.

Optional module

With [[opt-heart-seals]], reaching A+ or S rank with another unit adds one of their classes to your own list.

Weapons can be modified upon their purchase, if the GM decides that a forge is available at the time. The GM may also allow existing weapons to be forged at their discretion.

What can be changed

Once a weapon has been purchased and selected to be forged, the following stats can be changed in set increments, up or down.

StatEach incrementLimit
Might1+5 or −5
Hit5+25 or −25
Critical3+15 or −15
Weight1+5 or −5

As each stat can be modified up to 5 times, the total number of modifications a weapon can have is 20.

What it costs

Each modification applied to a weapon costs gold, and each increment costs more than the last: 150 gold × the increment level. All costs are in addition to the base weapon cost.

To increase a weapon stat all the way up to 5 increments, it would cost 2,250 gold. To increase all stats all the way up to 5 increments, it would cost 9,000 gold in addition to the base weapon cost.

Key rule

Staves are not considered weapons for the purposes of forging, so their stats cannot be modified as such. Laguz weapons and Manakete and Taguel Stones cannot be forged either.

The Convoy is the stock of additional weapons and items that players obtain, but do not currently use.

With a Convoy, players can store gear in it for later, and will send excess items they do not have space in their inventories for to it. Without a Convoy, items must be discarded instead.

In order to make use of the Convoy, the GM must first set its use case. Convoy services are always available at a Base (unless stated otherwise by the GM), but on the field it is up to the GM as to how to proceed.

GM advice

Three ideas for the Convoy during a battle:

  • There is no way to access the Convoy in a battle, but it will still receive excess items.
  • A player or ally unit is designated as the Convoy access point, and units adjacent to them can store and retrieve items in the Convoy.
  • Players must visit a Convoy building on the map, like an Armory or Vendor, in order to store or retrieve items, and may be charged a fee to do so.

Fire Emblem Online is a very, very combat-focused system, it is true. However, that does not mean non-combat encounters and off-map roleplay are impossible.

GM advice

Anything and everything outside of a map can be turned into a memorable scene, and GMs are encouraged to come up with things other than fighting to do for their players, so that both parties might be allowed to exercise their roleplaying muscles. Shopping trips, interrogations, infiltrations, strategy meetings, casual conversations, games of chance, anything you can think of, really.

[[ability-checks|Ability Checks]], too, will help a lot with breathing life into the time between fights. Of course, on-map roleplay is not impossible either. Anytime, anywhere, can be a stage. It is just up to the GM and the players to make it so.

The Base

Between battles players may be returned to a home base rather than moving right on towards the next battle. This can be anything from a castle, a city, an encampment, even a single house.

At your Base you may have a variety of things to do, such as access to shops to buy items from, NPCs to talk to and other events and activities the GM can come up with. [[convoy|Convoy]] access is always available at the Base, but activities and shops must be crafted by GMs.

Sparring and Training

One idea for an activity at bases is Sparring, where two player units may face off against one another for experience.

  • The number of times this can be done depends on your GM, but is by default 3 times per unit with a usable staff.
  • Two units engaged in training will use their own weapons, and both receive kill EXP calculated against their opponent's level, with the loser receiving +10 extra EXP by default. See [[exp]].
  • Units who will heal the combatants after battle may select what staff to use, so that they can modulate the EXP and wEXP gained at their leisure.

This means that a level 10 unit soundly defeating a level 5 unit will greatly increase the EXP of the level 5 unit, and lesser level gaps will give both units some extra EXP and wEXP.
