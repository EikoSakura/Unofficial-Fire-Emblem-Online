Adds the ability to perform several Combat Arts with various types of weapons to improve combat performance.

Optional module

This page covers the rules for Combat Arts. The list of Arts themselves is not part of this journal. See [[opt-more]].

Using a Combat Art

Units may perform Combat Arts when initiating combat. Depending on the GM's preference, they will either make a singular attack using the Combat Art, or will make any follow-ups as normal, with their first attack (unless specified otherwise) carrying the Combat Art's effects.

  • Combat Arts with 2x Strikes do not stack with 2x Strikes from other sources, such as Brave Weapons, and Combat Arts only apply to the first attack made with 2x Strikes.
  • Dodge and Critical Avoid apply to the first attack by an enemy unless otherwise specified.
  • If able to follow-up, Combat Arts that modify range allow their user to follow-up as though they had the extended range, although they lose their other effects.

Costs

Combat Arts have Costs to use, taking HP or Weapon Uses.

CostRule
HPCombat Arts cannot be used if the unit would die from the HP cost. Laguz, Taguel and Manakete weapons can execute Combat Arts that cost HP.
Weapon UsesThey may be used if the weapon would break from the Weapon Use cost, with the weapon breaking after combat as normal. Broken Weapons cannot execute Arts with Weapon Use costs, and neither can Laguz, Taguel and Manakete weapons.

Learning Combat Arts

Units gain a Combat Art every time they achieve a new Weapon Rank, the learned Combat Art being of equal Rank or lower.

  • For E Rank, this means acquiring an E Rank Universal Art.
  • Starting at D and up, units can choose to gain weapon-specific arts, only usable with that weapon type.
  • All Universal Arts may be used with any weapon once learned, regardless of the Ranks of the Art and Proficiency in question.

Which weapons use which Arts

Weapons that count as other types use the type they count as for what Combat Arts they can use.

Battalions are squadrons led by a commanding unit which can provide several useful bonuses, and special actions called Gambits.

Optional module

The Battalions listed on the Optional Content Sheet are designed to be applicable to most campaigns, but GMs are heavily encouraged to make their own to better fit theirs.

What a Battalion gives

When equipping a Battalion, it can provide a number of effects, such as increasing the unit's STR, MAG, DEF, RES, LUK, Hit, Critical, Dodge, Critical Avoid, Attack Speed, LoS, and even CHA. Battalions also all have special actions that can be taken, called Gambits.

Gambits

Gambits have many different effects.

  • All Offensive Gambits inflict the Rattled status on hit, which decreases STR, MAG, DEF, RES, DEX, SPD and MOV by 2, and prevents the target from using Gambits of their own, as well as choosing when to activate Skills and Combat Arts. Rattled wears off after one turn.
  • Gambits cannot be counter-attacked, and cannot make follow-up attacks.
Gambit damage
Might + STR or MAG + CHA ÷ 5 − Foe's DEF or RES
Gambit to-hit target number
Hit + 5 × ( CHA − Foe's CHA )

The CHA part is capped at +30 or −30.

Authority

Battalions and Gambits also introduce a new proficiency essential to using them: Authority. Authority starts either at F or E rank, depending on the GM's preference. Authority increases your ability to equip better battalions, and also minorly increases your chances to hit or avoid Gambits.

Key rule

For each rank in Authority, you gain +5 to hit and avoid for Gambits.

Battalion health

When the commanding unit takes damage, the Battalion takes 50% of the damage to their HP. The damage they take is capped at 50% of their Max HP, meaning they cannot be defeated in one round.

When defeated, Battalions retreat and must be replenished. Replenishing a Battalion can occur any time between battles, such as at a Base or during Preparations.

Special Qualities

Battalions have Special Qualities that affect a few things.

  • Effective weapons boost damage dealt to the Battalion by 1.5×.
  • Battalions with the Flying, Mounted, Terror, Beast or Laguz Special Quality cannot be assigned to units who lack the quality.

Levels

Gambits also have levels, leveled 1 to 5. Each level is 10 EXP, and you get 1 EXP for using a Gambit, and 3 EXP for defeating another unit or Battalion with your Battalion. When a Battalion levels up, it gains a fixed amount of stats, listed as “per level up”.

This module adds Breaks. Landing a hit with Weapon Triangle Advantage takes away the foe's counterattack.

Optional modules

Break and the extended triangle can be used together or separately. The core rules are in [[weapon-triangle]].

Break

When a unit with Weapon Triangle Advantage initiates combat and lands an attack that deals at least 1 damage, the foe with Disadvantage suffers from Break.

Key rule

Units afflicted with Break cannot counterattack during that combat and the next, or until the start of their next turn, whichever comes first.

  • Break is not a proper status condition that can be cured.
  • This applies to units of all factions, meaning enemies can Break the players, and vice versa.
  • Armored units are immune to Break.

The extended triangle

In addition to this (or separately), if desired, is an extended Weapon Triangle incorporating Bows, Brawl, Knives and others, in order for them to have the ability to Break as well. Each arrow points from the winner to the loser.

Units can choose to Capture enemies instead of killing them.

Optional module

A captured unit is carried like a Rescued one. See [[actions|Rescuing]].

Attempting a capture

Key rule

To capture a unit, one must have higher Weight than them. Capturing works like a normal round of combat, however all of the capturing unit's stats except LUK are temporarily halved.

Afterwards·Enemies stunned like this cannot be cured by any means, and regain control and their previous health if they are uncaptured by the end of the next turn.

Who cannot be captured

  • Beorc Mounted and Flying units, unless they have certain conditions or are dismounted.
  • Ballisticians.
  • Shifted Laguz.
  • Manakete and Taguel, if they still have transformations remaining.
  • Terror units.

Automatic captures

Under certain circumstances, combat is skipped entirely and the target is automatically captured. Those are, if the unit:

  • is afflicted with Sleep, Stun, Petrification, Freeze, Beckon, Seduce, Stop or Control;
  • has no weapons in their inventory;
  • has only broken weapons or staves in their inventory; or
  • can only fight back with innate Fists.

Holding a captive

  • Units can trade with captured units, such as for taking equipment from them.
  • A unit can release their captive at any time, in which case they leave the map and are no longer a combatant.
  • Defeating a unit with a captive will free the captive, and they will return to combat with the amount of HP they had before being captured.

When this module is active, all units gain a Stagger bar, equal to 60% of their current HP.

Optional module

Stagger adds a second bar to track beside HP for every unit on the map.

How it works

  1. Damage lowers the bar

    Taking damage lowers the Stagger bar by the amount of damage taken. Units do not take Stagger damage during combat they initiate.

  2. An empty bar Staggers the unit

    When a unit's Stagger bar is depleted, they take double damage from all sources and are unable to act for the rest of the turn and until the end of their next.

  3. The bar comes back

    Stagger is not restored through HP healing. Instead it is restored by 20% of its max value at the start of the unit's turn, and refills to max after the unit recovers from being Staggered.

Eight short modules that each change a single rule of combat. A GM may use any of them on its own.

Optional modules

The core rules these change are in [[combat]] and [[weapons]].

Follow-up Critical Multiplier

Follow-up Critical Multiplier, or FCM, lets units land critical hits more on follow-up attacks.

  • FCM can reach a value of up to 5, and the base is 1. An FCM of 1 means you do not gain a bonus.
  • On follow-up attacks, the unit's Critical is multiplied by their FCM, so a 5 becomes a 10 if the unit's FCM is 2.
  • Brave effect weapons are exempt from this except in scenarios where they will hit 4 times from doubling, in which case the third and fourth attacks (technically the second) will receive the FCM.
  • It is up to the GM's discretion if player units can get or start with an FCM higher than 1.

FE4 Criticals

Makes critical hits deal 2× damage before defense, instead of 3× damage after defense. Weapons with effects like making criticals do 4× instead of 3× will instead do 3× instead of 2× before defense.

FE4 Effectiveness

Effective damage does not multiply weapon Might, but instead deals a standard critical hit. These “Criticals” are not nullified by item or skill effects that negate Criticals, only ones that negate Effective Damage.

Minimum Damage

If a unit negates damage from Defense or Resistance, the attack instead deals 1 damage. This does not affect skills like Iron Wall, which will still cause no damage to be dealt.

Double Roll RNG

The GM can decide to use the Double Roll system, where 2 numbers are rolled for Hit and the average between them (rounded down) is the actual result.

FE5 RNG

Hit chances cannot go below 1% and can only go to 99%, always leaving at least a 1% margin of hitting and dodging.

Weapon Kill Critical Bonus

With this module, units who use a specific weapon for a long time will eventually gain benefits from it. Namely, critical chance.

When reaching 50 kills with a specific weapon (individual or collective, that is, all Iron Swords or just the one), units will gain +1 Critical while using that weapon for every kill past 50, up to 100 kills for a total of +50 Critical.

Depending on the availability of repairs in your campaign, it may be worthwhile to consider adjusting these values.

Berwick Counters/Doubles

With this module, combat gains much more focus on landing the first strike.

Key rule

When a unit lands an attack, the foe's next attack is cancelled. This is ignored if the damage dealt is zero, either naturally or after things like Shields, Skills, or otherwise.

Other distinct skill interactions include:

  • Cancel does not exist.
  • Deathmatch, Desperation and Reckless do not cancel attacks on hit while they are active.
  • Opportunist does not activate if a foe's attack is cancelled this way.

The rules add that there are probably more interactions still to be listed.

Two modules that give certain units Stars: Leadership Stars strengthen the units under a leader, and Movement Stars give a unit a chance to act twice.

Optional modules

These are two modules. A GM may use either one without the other.

Authority & Leadership

The GM can assign a Leadership value to non-playable units if they so wish. Leaders can have as low as 0 and up to 5 Leadership Stars by default.

  • Each star gives a bonus of either +10 Hit and Dodge to affected units in a 3-space range, or +3 Hit and +5 Dodge to all affected units on the map.
  • Units affected are units who have the unit as their “Authority”.
  • Units also gain their own leadership bonuses.
  • Units with Leadership Stars but a different Authority (usually a unit with a superior amount of stars) will still get their own bonuses, stacking with the Authority's bonuses.
  • The GM may also let players take advantage of this at their discretion.

Source note

The rules document words this as “+3 or +5 to all affected units on the map” without saying what the numbers are added to. They are +3 Hit and +5 Dodge, as the line above now reads.

Movement Stars

Movement Stars are a stat that allows a unit to act again after taking an action, like the Encore skill.

  • A unit typically may only have up to 5 Movement Stars.
  • Each star gives a 5% chance to give the unit another action, up to 25%.
  • Movement Stars can proc infinitely.
